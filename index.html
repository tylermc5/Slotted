The problem

Why every dollar you spent on creator marketing got you nothing you could measure.

The problem isn't that creator marketing doesn't work. It does. The problem is the deal structure. You pay regardless of outcome.

Yelp ads, Instagram boosts, third-party delivery promos, agency retainers, horizontal influencer platforms, one-off creator deals — every one of them collects your money whether a single new diner walks through your door. They're getting paid for impressions, content, or "reach." You're paying for hope.

Tabletop is the first program where the deal is restructured. You pay only when a verified new diner sits down. Below a contracted cover floor, you don't pay at all. The risk lives with us and our creators, not you.

That's not creator marketing. That's diner acquisition.

What we look for in Tabletop creators.

Hyperlocal focus

You post food content in a specific city or region. Hyperlocal creators win on this platform. National food creators are great for cookbook deals; they don't drive foot traffic in any one ZIP code.

Conversion, not just engagement

We sort creators by one number above all others: covers-per-thousand-impressions on food content. Save rate matters. Comments that include "trying this!" matter. Follower count is one input among many.

Craft

Quality matters. Lighting, sound, narrative. We're not asking for high production value — authenticity beats production every time — but we are asking for craft.

Long-term mindset

Tabletop only works if creators stay across multiple restaurants over multiple months. Network pooling compounds the longer you're in. If you're shopping for the highest single-post rate, we're not your platform.

What we don't do

  • Negotiate you down on rate
  • Send 12-page contracts
  • Ask for rights in perpetuity
  • Pay late, ever (Stripe Connect daily)
  • Sort creators by follower count
  • Match you with brands you wouldn't pick
