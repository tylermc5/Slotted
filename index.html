How a slot becomes a spot

List. Produce. Resell.

No new tools to learn. Slotted plugs into Google Ads + YouTube Studio — and prices every booking to the view curve the buyer actually captures.

01 · LIST

Inventory and capacity enter the exchange

Connect YouTube Studio. Every DBS-eligible slot — new uploads and back catalog — surfaces as priced inventory. Publish your endorsement profile: verticals, turnaround, capacity. Yield curves built from real transactions.

02 · BRIEF & PRODUCE

Buyer briefs. Creator produces. Both approve.

Buyers submit a structured brief — brand, key messages, CTA, audience. Creator records the read in their voice, often delivering 2–3 versions. Both approve. Escrow funds on signature. Single-shot or packaged into a 6/12mo contract.

Performance syncs to Google Ads in real time. CPM is shown for the buyer's actual slot duration (30/60/90-day, or packaged). Swap between produced versions in two clicks. FAQ

Questions we get a lot.

How is Slotted different from existing creator-marketing platforms?

Existing platforms — influencer-marketing software, creator-integration buying platforms, and programmatic ad-tech — all share one constraint: their data models were built before Dynamic Brand Segments existed. They treat the campaign or the integration as the unit of trade, which means swap, geo multi-tenancy, back-catalog inventory, and multi-integration packaging require retrofitting onto a shape they weren't designed for (typically 12–24 months of rebuild). Slotted is slot-shaped from day one. Public clearing prices, native Google Ads sync, duration-aware CPM, and the secondary-market mechanics are first-class primitives, not features bolted on. The exchange model also brings buyer-side benefits the legacy platforms can't price for — pay-for-the-curve-you-capture CPM and standardized exchange contracts instead of bespoke per-deal terms.

Who actually creates the ad?

The creator does — always. The buyer submits a brief (brand, product, key messages, CTA, audience screens). The creator records the read in their own voice, against the brief, often delivering 2–3 versions. Both sides approve before go-live. The creative is never brand-supplied. The trust transfer from creator to brand is the entire reason DBS clears at 3–5× display CPMs.

How does CPM work on a DBS slot? Do I get the long view-curve tail?

You get the view curve you commit to. DBS slots are dynamic: when your term ends, the slot reopens to the next buyer. A 30-day slot gives you 30 days of impressions, priced on 30-day CPM. A 60- or 90-day slot lets you capture deeper into the view curve, with proportionally lower amortized CPM. Multi-integration packages (6/12 months across multiple integrations) capture the full 90-day curve and compound the trust curve as well, which is where the 55% YoY cumulative CPM reduction Agentio measured actually applies. Back-catalog inventory gives you a mature, steady view curve from day one. Slotted shows the CPM that matches the duration you actually buy — no vendor averaging, no implied tail you can't access.

What is multi-integration packaging?

By the 6th integration with the same creator, CTR is 1.8× higher and CVR is 1.9× higher than the first integration. Single-shot campaigns never capture this — brands cut spend before the trust curve unlocks. Slotted packages 6-month and 12-month commitments with the same creator into single contracts at a 10% discount, giving creators predictable production schedules and giving buyers the cumulative CPM compression (down 55% YoY on consistent spend) that only repetition unlocks.

What happens to back-catalog videos?

Every long-form video a creator has uploaded still has a 60-second slot. Back-catalog videos keep getting views via search, recommendations, and suggested-video surfacing — and those slots are sellable. When a new buyer books a back-catalog slot, the creator records a fresh read for that buyer. The endorsement is always brand-specific; what's reusable is the slot inventory and the audience attached to it, not the creative.

Is this just programmatic ads in disguise?

No. Slotted brings the buying motion of programmatic — public clearing prices, yield curves, geo tenancy, swap, native measurement — to a market where the creative is creator-produced and every booking is a direct deal with creator approval. Per Google's own documentation, DBS inventory is not open-exchange programmatic. The motion is exchange-cleared. The creative isn't.

What is a Dynamic Brand Segment, exactly?

DBS is a new YouTube format (launching April 2026, US geo-targeting May 2026) that turns the 60-second creator-read sponsorship inside a long-form video into a standardized, swappable, geo-targeted, resellable slot. Google built the delivery and measurement rails. They explicitly opted out of the transaction layer. Slotted is the transaction layer.

Does Slotted replace my agent or manager?

No. Slotted is the marketplace and software layer. Strategy, relationships, and bespoke deals that don't fit the slot model still belong to humans. Most creators keep both.

How do payments work?

Brands fund escrow when a brief is approved. We release funds when the read goes live in YouTube Studio. Payouts run through Stripe Connect with standard creator payment terms.

What's the minimum spend?

Self-serve starts at $10K per campaign. Trading-desk seat and enterprise sales accounts have no minimum. Most first campaigns clear between $50K and $250K. Multi-integration packages typically start at $150K for 6 months.
